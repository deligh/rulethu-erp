Market watch

Market Context: Gold is falling today primarily due to a strengthening US dollar and shifting expectations surrounding Federal Reserve monetary policy. The dollar remains firm, making dollar-priced bullion more expensive for international buyers, while the market prices in potential interest rate hikes later this year. The US dollar has held firm near a one-year high. When the dollar strengthens, gold (which is priced in USD) becomes costlier for holders of other currencies, dampening overall demand. Following recent policy discussions, several Federal Reserve members have adopted a more hawkish tone. Traders are increasingly pricing in the possibility of an interest rate hike before the end of the year to combat inflation. Gold does not yield interest or pay dividends. When central banks keep interest rates elevated or hint at further hikes, yield-bearing assets (like US Treasury bonds) become more attractive to investors, causing them to rotate away from gold. After massive rallies in precious metals over the past year, many investors are choosing to lock in their gains, leading to increased selling pressure in the paper gold and ETF markets.
